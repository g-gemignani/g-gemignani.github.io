## Current work

Today I am Head of Software at 24 Industries, a startup that builds uncrewed aerial systems. The work spans drone software and Ground Control Stations: architecture and autonomy, simulation, quality assurance, and compliance with aerospace standards. It also covers the less glamorous but essential parts of the job: making behavior reliable, integrating software with hardware constraints, hiring, and keeping an engineering organization understandable as it grows.

## Background

Before that I spent years in logistics robotics, where I led a team of more than 20 engineers responsible for robot architecture design, robot behavior development, simulation, and product quality assurance. Across all these years I have developed mainly in Python, C++, and Prolog, on ROS.

My path into robotics started in physics, continued through artificial intelligence, and eventually settled in the space where reasoning, perception, interaction, and execution meet. During my PhD in Robotics and Artificial Intelligence at Sapienza University of Rome, and through a visiting period at Carnegie Mellon University, I worked on topics such as semantic mapping, human-robot interaction, task acquisition, and knowledge representation.

## What I care about

During my studies I developed a deep interest in artificial intelligence and robotics, and above all in the cognitive side of the subject. One question has fascinated me since: how can a machine, a single robot or a multi-robot system, be given intelligent behavior that lets it learn and reason about how to act in a complex world? That question holds others that humankind needs, at least, to attempt to answer. What do we mean by the adjective intelligent? What gives a specific entity that attribute? Will we ever be able to instill that trait in a machine? I would like to spend my life contributing to the answers.

In practice, that interest pulls me toward approaches that balance expressive models with operational robustness: systems that are not only smart on paper, but useful and dependable when deployed. Over the years it has taken different forms: research papers, patents, European projects, open-source contributions, and product-facing engineering work. This site is meant as a compact record of that trajectory: selected projects, publications, and a few of the ideas that have shaped how I think about robotics.
